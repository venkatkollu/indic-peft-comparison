Introduction

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods — LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), its variant DoRA (Liu et al., 2024), and IA³ (Liu et al., 2022) — have become the default approach for adapting large pretrained language models to downstream tasks under limited compute and memory budgets. Each modifies a frozen backbone differently: LoRA injects low-rank additive updates into attention weight matrices; DoRA decomposes those updates into separate magnitude and direction components, aiming to more closely approximate full fine-tuning's learning dynamics; IA³ instead learns a small number of per-layer rescaling vectors, updating far fewer parameters than either LoRA variant. Comparative studies of these methods typically report a fixed ranking — usually favoring LoRA or DoRA — but this ranking is generally established under a single, specific experimental condition: one dataset size, one language, one script.

Two recent comparative studies (Frontiers in Big Data, 2025; PROPOR, 2026) evaluate LoRA, DoRA, and IA³ against one another, but share two limitations relevant to this work. First, both restrict their language coverage to Latin-script languages — English and Brazilian Portuguese — leaving open whether their reported method rankings hold for languages with different scripts, morphological structures, and tokenization behavior under multilingual encoders. Second, both evaluate at a single fixed training-set size, leaving open whether the reported ranking is a property of the methods themselves or an artifact of the particular data regime tested. Given that annotation budgets in real-world low-resource NLP settings routinely range from tens to tens of thousands of examples, a ranking established at one dataset size provides limited guidance for practitioners operating anywhere else on that spectrum.

This gap is particularly consequential for Indic languages. India's official languages span multiple distinct script families and language families — Devanagari and Indo-Aryan (e.g., Hindi), Telugu script and Dravidian (e.g., Telugu), among others — and multilingual encoders such as XLM-RoBERTa are known to tokenize these languages with substantially different subword fragmentation rates than English, which can affect how efficiently PEFT adapters exploit a fixed effective sequence length. Whether a PEFT method ranking derived from English/Portuguese evaluation transfers to this setting is an empirical question, not a safe assumption.

We address this gap directly. Using XLM-RoBERTa-base fine-tuned on IndicXNLI, we compare LoRA, DoRA, and IA³ on two Indic languages selected specifically for their script and family contrast — Hindi (Devanagari, Indo-Aryan) and Telugu (Telugu script, Dravidian) — across six training budgets spanning three orders of magnitude (50 to 20,000 examples) and three random seeds, for a total of 108 trained configurations. This design lets us ask two questions no prior study has answered jointly: (1) does the relative ranking of these three PEFT methods change as annotation budget changes, and (2) does whatever ranking emerges hold consistently across two languages from different script families, or is it script-dependent? Our results (Section [Results]) show a clear, budget-dependent, and cross-script-consistent ranking — IA³ outperforms both LoRA and DoRA once training data exceeds roughly 500 examples, in both languages — challenging the assumption that a PEFT method ranking established under one language and one data regime generalizes broadly.
