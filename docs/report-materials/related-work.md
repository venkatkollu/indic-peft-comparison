Related Work

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning. LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) is the most widely adopted PEFT method, injecting trainable low-rank matrices into frozen attention projections and requiring only a small fraction of a model's parameters to be updated. DoRA (Liu et al., 2024) extends this by decomposing pretrained weights into magnitude and direction components, updating each separately, with the goal of more closely tracking full fine-tuning's optimization behavior while retaining LoRA's parameter efficiency. IA³ (Liu et al., 2022) takes a distinct approach, learning per-layer rescaling vectors applied to keys, values, and feedforward activations rather than injecting new weight matrices — resulting in substantially fewer trainable parameters than either LoRA variant. These three methods are frequently benchmarked against one another and against full fine-tuning, typically on English-language classification or generation tasks.

PEFT comparison studies. Direct comparisons of LoRA, DoRA, and IA³ have generally been conducted on Latin-script languages at a single fixed training-set size (Frontiers in Big Data, 2025; PROPOR, 2026), reporting a specific method ranking under those conditions. This leaves two open questions our work addresses: whether that ranking is sensitive to the amount of available training data, and whether it is sensitive to the script or morphological structure of the target language. Broader PEFT literature has separately studied data-efficiency effects (comparing PEFT methods at varying dataset sizes within a single language) and cross-lingual transfer effects (comparing a single PEFT method across languages), but to our knowledge these two axes have not been jointly varied in a controlled comparison of LoRA, DoRA, and IA³.

Multilingual encoders and Indic NLP. XLM-RoBERTa (Conneau et al., 2020) is a widely used multilingual encoder pretrained across 100 languages, including Hindi and Telugu, and serves as a common backbone for cross-lingual transfer research. Benchmark suites such as IndicGLUE and IndicXTREME (Kakwani et al., 2020; Doddapaneni et al., 2023) have substantially expanded the availability of labeled evaluation data for Indic languages, including IndicXNLI (Aggarwal et al., 2022), a machine-translated extension of XNLI covering eleven Indic languages including Hindi and Telugu, which we use as the evaluation task in this work. Prior work using these resources has generally focused on establishing baseline model performance or comparing full fine-tuning against zero-shot cross-lingual transfer, rather than comparing PEFT methods against one another within this language setting.

Tokenization and script effects. Subword tokenizers trained primarily on Latin-script, high-resource corpora are known to fragment non-Latin-script and morphologically rich languages into a larger number of subword units per word, which can affect effective sequence length, training efficiency, and downstream task performance. Our own tokenizer fragmentation analysis (Section [Methodology]) quantifies this effect directly for Hindi and Telugu under XLM-RoBERTa's tokenizer, motivating our choice to test PEFT methods on two languages with markedly different fragmentation profiles rather than treating "Indic languages" as a single uniform setting.

Positioning of this work. Unlike prior PEFT comparisons, we vary training budget across three orders of magnitude and evaluate on two languages from different script and language families, allowing us to test whether a PEFT method ranking is a stable property of the methods or contingent on the specific data regime and language under study.
