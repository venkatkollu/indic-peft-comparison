Abstract

We investigate whether the relative performance ranking of three parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods — LoRA, DoRA, and IA³ — holds across script families and training-data budgets, extending prior comparisons that were limited to Latin-script languages at a single fixed dataset size. Using XLM-RoBERTa-base fine-tuned on IndicXNLI, we evaluate all three methods on Hindi (Devanagari, Indo-Aryan) and Telugu (Telugu script, Dravidian) across six budgets (50 to 20,000 examples) and three random seeds (108 total configurations). We find that IA³ consistently outperforms LoRA and DoRA once training data exceeds 500 examples, in both languages — a crossover that replicates across two typologically distinct scripts — while LoRA and DoRA remain statistically indistinguishable from one another at every budget tested. IA³'s advantage is accompanied by higher variance at the smallest budgets (50–100 examples), where 9 of 108 runs failed to exceed random-chance accuracy. These results suggest PEFT method rankings are not universal across data regimes, and that recommendations should be conditioned on annotation budget rather than treated as fixed.

Limitations
Capped "high-resource" budget. The largest training budget used (20,000 examples) is a compute-constrained substitute for the full IndicXNLI training set (~392,702 examples per language). Whether the observed IA³ advantage persists, narrows, or reverses at true full-data scale is untested. Any reference to "high-resource" or "large-budget" performance in this work should be read as bounded by 20,000 examples, not the complete dataset.
Pooled, not per-language, hyperparameter search. Learning rates were selected once per method using data pooled across both languages, rather than tuned separately per language. This was a deliberate methodological choice to prevent language-specific LR tuning from confounding the cross-language ranking comparison — the central question this study addresses. A consequence is that reported performance for each method may not represent that method's absolute best-achievable result in either language individually.
Small-budget instability, concentrated in IA³. 13 of 108 runs (12%) failed to exceed random-chance accuracy; 9 of these occurred in IA³ at the two smallest budgets (50 and 100 examples). We attribute this to IA³'s selected learning rate (5×10⁻³) being fifty times larger than LoRA/DoRA's (1×10⁻⁴), which appears to occasionally destabilize training when very few gradient-informative examples are available. These runs are retained in the reported means and confidence intervals rather than discarded, so budget-500 and below results for IA³ should be read with this variance in mind (full listing in results/collapsed_runs.csv).
Two languages, two methods' worth of adapters. Findings are drawn from one Indo-Aryan and one Dravidian language, both processed through the same multilingual encoder (XLM-RoBERTa-base) and the same NLI task (IndicXNLI). Generalization to other Indic languages, other script families, other base encoders, or other task types (e.g., generation, sequence labeling) is not established by this study.
Classification head shared across methods. All three PEFT configurations train the same randomly initialized classification head alongside their respective adapters (modules_to_save=["classifier"]). It remains possible that this shared, jointly-trained component compresses differences between methods that would otherwise be more visible — particularly relevant to the null result between LoRA and DoRA — and this was not isolated as a separate experimental factor.
Single base model. All experiments use XLM-RoBERTa-base exclusively. Method rankings observed here may not transfer to larger multilingual encoders, decoder-only architectures, or encoders with different multilingual pretraining compositions.
